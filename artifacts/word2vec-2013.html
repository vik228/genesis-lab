03

The two objectives

Same corpus, same tree, same tables - the only difference is which side of the (centre, context) pair does the predicting, and that difference is what section 10 turns out to hinge on.

Skip-gram

Centre predicts context. One (centre, context) pair is one training example. word_emb[centre] is updated; the context word goes through node_emb along its Huffman path.

CBOW

Averaged context predicts centre. One whole window is one training example. word_emb of every context word is updated at 1/(2R) each; the centre goes through node_emb.

The spine of this page In CBOW a word's evaluated vector is only ever updated from windows where it sits in context. Its own occurrence as a centre never touches word_emb - that pass through the network only ever writes to node_emb, which gets thrown away.
04

Hierarchical softmax over the Huffman tree

Softmax over V = 71,290 becomes a walk down a binary tree: one sigmoid per node on the path, loss is masked BCE over that path. The tree is built once, on the original counts - subsampling in section 06 never touches it.

One softmax call becomes one tree walk
P(w | h) = Πnode ∈ path(w) σ( ± node_embnode · h )    sign flips per left/right turn
loss = − Σnode masknode · [ code·log σ(x) + (1−code)·log(1−σ(x)) ]
depth: min 4 · max 22 · frequency-weighted avg 10.65    a balanced tree would average 16.12 - frequent words get short codes, which is where the speedup comes from
zero-learning baseline = avg depth × ln 2 ≈ 10.65 × 0.693 ≈ 7.38 nats    expected loss if every sigmoid sits at 0.5 - the number a healthy run should fall well below

Radius redrawn fresh per centre, uniform on {1..5} - not a schedule, not per-epoch. Three independent draws for the same centre would rarely look this different, but they can.

So one pass = 16,718,844 × 6 = 100,313,064 pairs, /16 = 6,269,566 steps for skip-gram. CBOW is one example per token: 16,718,844 / 16 = 1,044,927 steps.

6x is a real asymmetry, not a bug By symmetry a word also lands in ~6 other words' windows, so both architectures give a word 6 × count(w) word_emb updates per pass. The counts are equal between the two objectives - only what each update contains differs. The step-count gap is purely a batching artifact of skip-gram exploding one window into 2R separate examples.

Subsampling (from 2013b)

A per-occurrence coin flip on the training stream only - vocab and the Huffman tree both stay built on the original, unsubsampled counts.

Keep probability
keep(w) = min( 1, √(t / f(w)) )    t = 1e-5, f(w) = count(w) / n, redrawn every pass

07

Negative sampling (from 2013b)

The other half of 2013b. Subsampling thins the stream; negative sampling replaces the output layer outright - and gives up being a probability model to do it.

A softmax denominator does quiet work: raise one word's score and every other word's probability falls automatically, because they all share the same normaliser. Section 04's tree keeps that property while making it cheap. Negative sampling deletes the denominator instead, and with it that automatic downward pressure - so the pressure has to be supplied by hand, by naming a few words and saying "not this one". That is the entire purpose of the negatives.

One V-way classification becomes k+1 independent yes/no questions. No competition between slots, no argmax, no winner - every slot gets its own sigmoid and every one contributes to the loss.

Objective and noise distribution
L = − log σ( vpos · h ) − Σi=1..k log σ( −vneg i · h )    the minus inside the second σ is what pushes those scores down
= binary cross-entropy over k+1 slots, labels [1, 0, 0, …, 0]    the same masked-BCE shape as section 04, with word rows instead of tree nodes and no mask needed
Pnoise(w) = count(w)0.75 / Σ count0.75    exponent is empirical - the paper gives no derivation
zero-learning baseline = (k+1) × ln 2  →  4.16 at k=5, 11.09 at k=15    the NS analogue of the 7.38 checkpoint; matched to six decimals at init

Why 0.75 and not 1.0: under the plain unigram almost every negative would be the or of, which the model separates immediately and then keeps re-learning. Under uniform, almost every negative would be an ultra-rare word it already separates. The exponent takes a square-ish root of the frequency differences, the same move subsampling makes in section 06.

Implementation follows word2vec.c: the distribution is pre-expanded into a 10M-entry table, so a draw is one randint plus one gather rather than a normalisation per call. Shapes are h (B, D), targets (B, k+1) with column 0 the true word, logits (B, k+1). A negative colliding with the true word is not checked for, as in the original - at V = 71,290 it is rare and costs one slot.

It stops being a language model Hierarchical softmax is a reparameterisation of the softmax: the probabilities still sum to 1. Negative sampling is not - nothing sums to anything. It is a discriminative surrogate that happens to produce usable vectors, which is why 2013b makes no perplexity claim for it.

10

Matched steps ≠ matched per-word updates

The subsampled run kept the same step budget the baseline used - but the stream feeding it was only 27.9% the size, so it silently made more passes over the surviving tokens.

Passes, and where they land
same step budget, stream = 27.9% of tokens  →  3.58 passes    1 / 0.279 ≈ 3.58
each word's update count scales by keep(w) × 3.58
break-even: keep(w) = 0.279  →  count = 2,146    passes = 1/survival when the baseline is one pass
count < 2,146  →  up to 3.58x MORE updates    named-entity words live here
count > 2,146  →  fewer updates, down to 0.04x for "the"
Confound moves levels, not gaps The experiment silently moved learning from frequent to rare words by up to 90x. That explains why both models fell in the most frequent decile (CBOW −17.6, skip-gram −11.1) - but it was identical for both models, and an identical cause cannot produce a difference between them. The named-entity vs common gap in section 09 survives this confound; only the absolute levels are suspect.

Negative sampling vs hierarchical softmax

2013b's headline is that NEG-5 beats HS-Huffman by 12 points on the analogy task. Three full epochs on text8, every setting matched except the head, say that claim is half right - and that the missing half is a precondition the paper never states.

SplitHSNS k=5NS k=15
semantic14.2%6.7%8.5%
syntactic15.2%11.4%18.2%
total14.8%9.4%14.2%

k = 5 was a misconfiguration. The paper recommends 5-20 negatives for small corpora and 2-5 for large ones; text8 is 16.7M words against their 1B, so 5 was the wrong end of the range. Raising it to 15 recovers nearly the whole 5.4-point deficit - and then the total stops being the interesting number, because NS k=15 beats HS on syntactic by 3.0 and loses on semantic by 5.7.

Per category the split is stark. Up: gram3-comparative 19.9 → 32.0, family 28.3 → 35.7, gram5-present-participle 13.8 → 19.7. Down: capital-common-countries 35.4 → 25.3, capital-world 12.0 → 5.0, city-in-state 13.3 → 7.2. It is the section 09 regrouping again, arrived at from a different direction.

Ten equal buckets, 1,783 questions each. Sign flips between min-count 79 and 130. Longest bars: HS +8.2, NS +7.9.
Why the crossover exists - shared nodes vs private rows A rare word gets a long Huffman code, and every node on that path is shared with other words, so under HS a rare word is trained partly by its neighbours in the tree. Under NS each word owns one private output row, updated only when the word itself occurs. Raising k makes the discrimination task harder and sharpens the words that have enough occurrences to benefit - it cannot manufacture data for the ones that do not.

Which retro-explains the paper. On 1B words, text8's rare words are 60x more frequent, so this weakness barely shows. The unstated precondition on the 12-point claim is not corpus size as such but absolute occurrence count per word.

not reproduced on the paper's own metric at this scale - 14.2% against HS 14.8%, a difference around the noise floor. reproduced on half the vocabulary NS wins clearly wherever the words have the counts to support it.

Loss is not the verdict, again NS drove its own objective far below its own baseline - mean diff near −2.15 per decile against HS's −0.63 - while producing worse vectors. Section 07's objective is a surrogate, and at k=5 an easy one: telling the true context from five random draws does not take much. The loss curve cannot rank these two heads.
12

Lessons

The failure modes that cost the most time re-implementing this paper from scratch.

LessonDetail
lr is per-exampleThe paper's lr 0.025 is a per-example rate. Under B=256 batch-mean it behaves like 0.025/256 and produces no measurable learning in 10,000 steps. Check batch size before transferring any pre-2012 published rate.
sweeps pick unstable ratesA short lr sweep picks unstable rates, not good ones. A 1000-step sweep ranked lr=2 as 2.7x better than lr=1; lr=2 diverged 1.1% into the epoch and lr=1 overtook it by 25k steps. Initial speed and stability are anti-correlated.
"absurd" ≠ "non-finite"An abort guard must trip on absurd, not on non-finite. A diverging run sits at finite losses like 1e26 for tens of thousands of steps. The guard used: loss > 5 × (avg huffman depth × ln2).
strip stopwords firstRaw neighbour entropy measures English, not the word: every word's neighbour list is ~19/20 identical to the corpus top-20 because stopwords dominate. Strip the top-100 first.
short probes fail on accuracy tooAlready known: short sweeps mislead on loss. They mislead on accuracy as well, for a different reason. HS reaches 14.8% over a full epoch but only 2.2% at one sixth of one on a fully compressed lr schedule - the gains arrive late. A 5-arm grid at 1M steps reproduced a +0.4 gap where the full epoch shows +5.4, and had to be discarded. Quality hypotheses here cost a full epoch each.
key runs by directory, not kindThe bucket-analysis script keyed its results on ck["kind"]. Two skip-gram runs - HS and NS - collided, the second silently overwrote the first, and every cell printed a +0.0 gap. It survived because the original use compared skip-gram against CBOW, where the kinds differ. A duplicate-label assert now guards it.
three ways to lose a runWrap long runs in caffeinate, disable AutomaticallyInstallMacOSUpdates, and launch inside tmux. Runs died to idle sleep, to an auto-update reboot at 67.8% that caffeinate could not stop, and twice to the parent session exiting - nohup did not save it. The check that does: the process should show PPID 1. setsid does not exist on macOS.
