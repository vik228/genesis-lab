01

Why BatchNorm breaks on variable-length RNNs

Batch Normalization (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015) normalizes each neuron across the mini-batch axis (dim = 0) and tracks an exponential moving average (running_mean = 0.9 * running_mean + 0.1 * batch_mean) for test time. In an RNN with variable-length sequences (say 3 sentences of lengths 3, 5, 8), across-batch statistics fail in three ways:

1. <pad> tokens corrupt batch and running statistics
Without masking, padded positions contribute synthetic zeros/constants to batch_mean and running_mean at every step after the shortest sequence ends.
2. Tail time steps have tiny effective batch size (sigma_t = 0)
Even if we mask <pad> and store separate mu_t, sigma_t per time step t, at steps t = 6, 7, 8 only Sentence 3 (len = 8) is active. With 1 active sentence, sigma_t = 0, and tail steps in any mini-batch suffer from noisy sample variance.
3. No stored statistics beyond maximum training length
If a test sentence arrives with length 12 > 8, no per-step running_mean_t or running_var_t exists for steps t = 9..12.

Equations, tensor shapes & why g, b have shape (H,)

In the table above, pure (a - mu) / sigma forces every row into [-1, +1], destroying the fact that Neuron 2 is 10x larger than Neuron 1. To let each neuron learn its own scale and baseline offset, LayerNorm applies learnable gain g and bias b of shape (H,):

Layer-Normalized RNN Step (Paper Section 3.1, Eq. 3-4)
Pre-activation
at = Whh ht-1 + Wxh xt
Per-example mean
mut = (1 / H) Σi=1..H at,i
Per-example std
sigmat = sqrt((1 / H) Σi=1..H (at,i - mut)2 + eps)
Normalized state
ht = f( (g / sigmat) ⊙ (at - mut) + b )
Why g, b are (H,) and not (B,): g and b are learned model parameters (nn.Parameter) shared across all examples and all time steps t. If they had shape (B,), the model could not run when test batch size changes, nor could it give Neuron 1 and Neuron 2 different scales.
Tensor / ParameterShapeRole in LayerNorm RNNCell
x_t, h_prev(B, D), (B, H)Current token input and previous hidden state at step t.
a_t(B, H)Summed recurrent + bottom-up pre-activations W_hh h_{t-1} + W_xh x_t.
mu, sigma(B, 1)Computed on the fly across dim=-1, keepdim=True for each example at step t.
self.g, self.b(H,)Learnable per-neuron gain (init 1) and bias (init 0), shared across B and T.
04

Why LayerNorm prevents exploding & vanishing RNN dynamics

In a Vanilla RNN, scaling W_hh by alpha compounds exponentially as alpha^T across T = 50 steps. LayerNorm cancels alpha at every single step in both the forward and backward passes:

Forward Scale Cancellation

Take a = [10, 100] (mu = 55, sigma = 45, output [-1, +1]). Multiply weights by 10x so a' = [100, 1000]: new mu' = 550 and new sigma' = 450 also scale by 10x, so (a' - mu') / sigma' = [-1, +1] is unchanged.

Backward Jacobian Cancellation

In BPTT, dh_t / dh_{t-1} = diag(1 - h_t^2) * (dLN(a_t) / da_t) * W_hh. When W_hh scales by alpha, the 1 / sigma_t factor inside dLN(a_t) / da_t scales by 1 / alpha, so alpha * (1 / alpha) = 1 at every step t.

Invariance Duality: Paper Section 5.1 (Table 1)

Every entry in Paper Table 1 (page 4) follows directly from whether a transformation scales/shifts entire columns (neurons) or entire rows (examples) of a = [[10, 100], [20, 200], [30, 300]]:

TransformationBatchNorm (Col-wise)LayerNorm (Row-wise)Concrete Check on [[10, 100], [20, 200], [30, 300]]
Weight matrix rescaling
W -> delta * W		 Invariant Invariant Every cell scales by delta; both row and column (mu, sigma) scale by delta and cancel.
Weight matrix re-centering
W -> W + 1 * gamma^T		 No Invariant For a single example x, every neuron in that row shifts by the same scalar gamma^T x, which cancels in a - mu_row.
Single weight vector rescaling
w_1 -> 10 * w_1		 Invariant No Scaling only Neuron 1 turns Row 1 [10, 100] into [100, 100]. Column 1 ([100, 200, 300]) cancels in BatchNorm, but Row 1 ([100, 100]) collapses to a - mu = [0, 0] in LayerNorm.
Dataset rescaling
X -> delta * X		 Invariant Invariant Scales the entire matrix a by delta; cancels in both.
Dataset re-centering
X -> X + 1 * Delta^T		 Invariant No Shifts Column i by constant w_i^T Delta across all examples (cancels in BatchNorm), but shifts Neuron 1 and Neuron 2 by different amounts inside a row.
Single training case rescaling
x_1 -> 10 * x_1		 No Invariant Scaling only Example 1 turns Row 1 into [100, 1000]. Row 1 cancels in LayerNorm ([-1, +1]), whereas Column 1 becomes [100, 20, 30] and distorts BatchNorm's batch stats.
