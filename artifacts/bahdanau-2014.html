Module 01 • Core Recurrence

Vanilla RNN, Time Unrolling & Shared Weights

An RNN is not $T$ separate layers—it is **a single cell** called repeatedly in a `for t in range(T):` loop using the **exact same weight matrices** ($W_{xh}, W_{hh}, W_{hy}$).

Batch-First Equations (Right-Mul):
$$h_t = \tanh(x_t W_{xh} + h_{t-1} W_{hh} + b_h)$$ $$\hat{y}_t = h_t W_{hy} + b_y$$

Exact Tensor & Weight Shapes

Hidden State $h_t$ & Bias $b_h$
Hidden-to-Output Weight $W_{hy}$

BPTT Shared Weights = Gradient Accumulation

Because $W_{xh}$ and $W_{hh}$ across every timestep $t=1 \dots T$ are **shared**, their gradient in Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) is the **sum** of each step contribution:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_{hh}} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_t}{\partial W_{hh}} \quad \text{where} \quad \frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-1}} = \text{diag}(1 - h_t^2) \cdot W_{hh}^T$$

💡 First-Principles Catch: In long sequences $W_{hh}^T$ gets multiplied again and again ($\prod_{k} W_{hh}^T$), which makes gradients vanish or explode!

Module 02 • Encoder-Decoder Architecture

Seq2Seq Decoupling & The Two Encoder Outputs

In a vanilla RNN every input token produces an output ($T_{in} = T_{out}$). In Machine Translation the input length ($T_{in}$) and output length ($T_{out}$) can differ — so we use **2 separate RNNs** (Encoder & Decoder).

Output #1: Memory Bank (On the Table)

$H_{enc} = \text{stack}([h_1, h_2, \dots, h_{T_{in}}])$

The hidden states of **all encoder timesteps** are stacked on `dim=1` and kept aside on the table.

⚠️ Crucial Clarification: This $(B, T_{in}, H)$ tensor does NOT enter the Decoder RNN cell directly as input! It waits outside for the Attention query.
Output #2: Final Summary State

$h_{T_{in}}$ (e.g., $h_3$) $\longrightarrow s_0$

Only the **last step** hidden state $h_{T_{in}}$ of the Encoder. This enters the first Decoder cell `[Dec 1]` as the initial hidden state **$s_0$**!

✅ Decoder Step 1 Input: Token $y_1 = \text{""}$ $(B, D)$ comes from below, and $s_0 = h_3$ $(B, H)$ comes from the left!

Module 03 • Dynamic Context & Batched 3D MatMul

Attention Mechanics & The $(B, 1, H)$ Reshape Trick

First `[Dec k]` runs like a normal RNN and produces its own $s_k \in (B, H)$. Then it turns that $s_k$ with `unsqueeze(1)` into **$(B, 1, H)$** so it can do a 3D Batched MatMul (`torch.bmm`) with the 3D tensor $H_{enc} \in (B, T_{in}, H)$ kept on the table!

Q&A "Where did $(B, 1, H)$ come from when the input was $(B, T_{in}, H)$?"

s₁ = tanh(y₁W_xh + s₀W_hh)

2. The unsqueeze(1) Trick
s₁_3d = s₁.unsqueeze(1)
Becomes 3D:
Because this is 1 decoder step!
3. Score MatMul (bmm)
α₁ = softmax(s₁_3d @ H_encᵀ)
× →
4. Context Weighted Sum
c₁ = (α₁ @ H_enc).squeeze(1)
× →

Module 04 • First-Principles Evolution of Scoring Functions

Why Not Just Raw $s_k \cdot h_i$? Luong vs. Bahdanau (Additive MLP)

We derived step by step what the problems with raw dot-product are, Two-Tower $\tanh(H_{enc}W_{nn})$ fails, and how the Bahdanau **6 matrices** work.

Issue #1: Raw $s_k \cdot h_i$

Dual-Responsibility & Dimension Conflict

  • Dimension Mismatch: If $H_{enc} = 256$ and $H_{dec} = 512$, then a direct dot product is impossible!
  • Memory Pollution: $s_k$ must remember Hindi grammar and $h_i$ must hold English context. If we force $s_k \approx h_i$ holds, the RNN own memory gets corrupted!
✅ Fix: Separate "Search ID Card" (Query/Key projections $W_s, W_h$) from "Raw Hidden Memory"!
Issue #2: $h_{nn} = \tanh(H_{enc}W_{nn})$

Why Both $s_k$ & $H_{enc}$ Must Enter $\tanh(\dots)$

  • Point 1 (Blind/Static): $\tanh(H_{enc}W_{nn})$ contains no $s_k$ at all! It cannot adapt "Bank" (money vs river) to the Decoder's current need ($s_k$).
  • Point 2 (Kundali / XOR Problem): The outer dot product ($s_k \cdot h_{nn}$) can only do "Same-to-Same" linear matching. Cross-feature rule ("One calm + other aggressive = Match") is learned only when both meet inside the same $\tanh$!
✅ Fix: $\tanh(s_{k-1}W_s + H_{enc}W_h) \cdot v$ (Bahdanau Additive MLP!)
Modern Era: Luong & Transformers

Projected Dot-Product ($Q K^T$) Wins on GPUs

Luong (2015) and Transformers (2017) said: keep the Bahdanau $W_s$ and $W_h$ projections (as $W_Q, W_K$), but remove $\tanh$ and vector $v$ and use a direct Dot Product:

$$\text{Score} = (s_k W_Q) \cdot (H_{enc} W_K)^T$$
🚀 Matrix multiplication (`bmm`) is 10x faster on Tensor Cores than 3D broadcasting + $\tanh$!

Bahdanau Additive Attention: Exact Broadcasting Trace

Notice how Bahdanau computes attention before running `[Dec k]`, using previous state $s_{k-1}$:

Step 1a (Decoder Query): $Q = (s_{k-1} \cdot W_s).\text{unsqueeze}(1)$
× →
Step 1b (Encoder Keys): $K = H_{enc} \cdot W_h$
× →
Step 2 (Broadcast Add + Tanh): $E = \tanh(Q + K)$
PyTorch copies $(B, 1, A)$ across all $T_{in}$ tokens automatically!
+ →
Step 3 (Project to 1 Scalar Score via $v$): $\text{Scores} = E \cdot v$
× →
Step 4 & 5 (Softmax & Weighted Sum): $c_k = \alpha_k^T \cdot H_{enc}$
× →

Total 6 Weight Matrices in Bahdanau Decoder

3 weights inside the Attention module to compute scores + 3 weights of the Decoder RNN cell itself:

A. Attention Module Weights (3)
1. Decoder State Projection $W_s$
2. Encoder States Projection $W_h$

4. Input Weight $W_{xh}^{(dec)}$ (for $[y_k; c_k]$)
5. Recurrent Weight $W_{hh}^{(dec)}$

Module 05 • Past + Future Context

Bidirectional RNN (Bi-RNN), Causality & State Bridging

"He went to the bank to deposit money" vs. "bank of the river"—until the later words are seen, `"bank"` cannot capture the correct meaning. So the Encoder runs **2 independent RNNs** (Forward + Backward)!

Bi-RNN Encoder Architecture: Two Independent Loops $\to$ Concat

Backward RNN (◄── Right to Left, Independent Weights W⃖_xh, W⃖_hh):
  ◄── h⃖₁  ◄── [Bwd 1] ◄── h⃖₂  ◄── [Bwd 2] ◄── h⃖₃  ◄── [Bwd 3] ◄── h⃖₄=0
                      ▲                        ▲                        ▲
                      │                        │                        │
Forward RNN (──► Left to Right, Independent Weights W⃗_xh, W⃗_hh):       │
h⃗₀=0 ──► [Fwd 1] ──► h⃗₁  ──► [Fwd 2] ──► h⃗₂  ──► [Fwd 3] ──► h⃗₃ 
            ▲                        ▲                        ▲
            │                        │                        │
       x₁="banks"               x₂="are"                 x₃="closed"
                                            

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Per-Word Concatenation (h_t = [h⃗_t ; h⃖_t]):
  • Word 1 ("banks"):  h₁ = [h⃗₁ ; h⃖₁] ──►    (h⃖₁ has seen "closed" → "are" → "banks"!)
  • Word 2 ("are"):    h₂ = [h⃗₂ ; h⃖₂] ──► 
  • Word 3 ("closed"): h₃ = [h⃗₃ ; h⃖₃] ──► 
  • Stacked Table Memory H_enc:        ──►   (Changes Bahdanau W_h to !)
Critical Constraint Causality / Autoregressive

Why Decoder Can NEVER Be Bidirectional

This is not just "not needed" — making the Decoder bidirectional is mathematically illegal:

1. At Inference (Testing) Time: When `[Dec 1]` is predicting the first word `"बैंक"` the future words (`"बंद"`, `"हैं"`) do not exist yet! What would a Backward RNN read right-to-left?
2. At Training Time (Data Leakage / Cheating): If a Backward RNN is run during training, then while predicting at Step 1 `"बैंक"` the Backward RNN would already see the target `"बैंक"` and just copy-paste without learning anything!
Architectural Bridge

How to Initialize Decoder $s_0 \in (B, H)$ from Bi-RNN?

The Encoder has two final states: the last Forward $\overrightarrow{h}_{T_{in}} \in (B, H)$ and the last Backward $\overleftarrow{h}_1 \in (B, H)$.

❌ Why Not Simple Average $\frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{h}_3 + \overleftarrow{h}_1)$? Both are built from different weight matrices ($\overrightarrow{h}_3$ focuses on sentence end, $\overleftarrow{h}_1$ on sentence start). Adding directly causes apples-to-oranges style destructive feature interference.
✅ Modern Bridge Layer ($W_{bridge}$): Concatenate both and project with a learnable matrix $W_{bridge}$ :
$$s_0 = \tanh\Big([\overrightarrow{h}_{T_{in}} \,;\, \overleftarrow{h}_1] \cdot W_{bridge}\Big) \longrightarrow (B, H)$$
(Note: Bahdanau's 2014 paper used only $\overleftarrow{h}_1 \cdot W_{init}$ since $\overleftarrow{h}_1$ just finished reading the 1st word!)
Module 06 • Variable Length Sequences

Padding Bugs, Masking & Dynamic Batching (`pack_padded_sequence`)

In real batches every sentence has a different length — to make `["banks","are","closed","<pad>","<pad>"]` into a rectangular we must pad with `<pad>`. This creates 3 silent bugs.

Bug 1: Attention Score Leakage

Why replace `<pad>` score with $-\infty$?

If `<pad>` score is kept at $0$, then Softmax gives $e^0 = 1$ and `<pad>` gets non-zero weight! So we insert $-\infty$ (`-1e9`) before Softmax:

$$e^{-\infty} = \frac{1}{e^\infty} = \mathbf{0.0}$$
Bug 2: Forward State Pollution

Sentence Ends Early ($t=3$ of $5$)

Sentence 1 (`["banks","are","closed","<pad>","<pad>"]`) has the true final state $h_3$. If the RNN runs at $t=4,5$, then $h_3 \to h_4 \to h_5$ gets polluted!

✅ Fix: $m_t = 0$ freeze $h_t = h_{t-1}$ (see Fix 1 below).
Bug 3: Backward RNN init

Corrupted Reverse Start ($\overleftarrow{h}_{\text{init}} \neq 0$)

While running backward ($t=5 \to 4 \to 3$), `"closed"` ($t=3$) should have received a clean $\mathbf{0}$ from the right, but it gets garbage $\overleftarrow{h}_4$ from 2 `<pad>` tokens!

Fix 1 Binary Mask Update (Without If-Else on GPU)

$$h_t = m_t \odot h_t^{\text{new}} \;+\; (1 - m_t) \odot h_{t-1}$$

• When the token is valid ($m_t = 1$): $h_t = h_t^{\text{new}}$ (Normal RNN update).

• When the token is `<pad>` ($m_t = 0$): $h_t = h_{t-1}$ (The old state $h_3$ or backward $\mathbf{0}$ is frozen/copied as-is!).

Fix 2: PyTorch pack_padded_sequence

Interactive Simulator: $B=10$ Batch (Indices 2, 3, 5 Shorter)

Instead of slicing `(1, H)` one by one at random indexes (`2, 3, 5`), sort descending and push short sequences to the bottom rows — each step runs a contiguous top slice `h[:B_t]`!


                

            

        



        
        
        
        

            

                Module 07 • Experiment Runbook
                
Toy Reverse Task: Debug & Analysis Flow, and What It Showed

                

                    Task: reverse a random token sequence (6-50 words + <eos>). Reversal is a hard case for a fixed context vector: the last output word is the first source word, which the encoder read up to 50 steps before the end and the decoder has to carry for up to 50 more. The flow below is the order we ran things in. Each step has a check that must pass before the next one means anything. The same flow is the plan for real data, with the substitutions in the blue "Real data" line of each card.
                

            


            
            

            
            


                

                    Steps 1-2 • Is the model wired right?
                    
Toy: the len-10 attention model gives an anti-diagonal heatmap (output step i looks at source position T+1-i). Greedy decode on those weights: 2000/2000 exact. Decode starts from a <sos> built with torch.full((B,), SOS), keeps a finished flag, writes <pad> after <eos>, and stops when all rows finish or at max_len.

                    
Side finding: the <eos> row of the heatmap is not sharp and varies by run (0.04 / 0.26 / 0.49). The decoder state already knows the last word was copied, so the loss never forces it to look at the source <eos>. Attention is sharp only where the output needs source information.

                    
Real data: overfit a tiny subset (about 1k pairs) to near-zero loss first. The heatmap on a known pair should be mostly monotonic, with local swaps where word order differs (adjective-noun in En-Fr).

                


                

                    Steps 3-4 • A fair baseline
                    
Toy: use_attention=False replaces $c_i$ with one fixed $c = [\overrightarrow{h}_T ; \overleftarrow{h}_1]$ of shape (B, 2H), computed once and fed wherever $c_i$ went (GRU input and deep output). Same Bi-GRU encoder, same decoder, so attention is the only difference. Params: 99,435 (attention) vs 97,484 (baseline); attention adds 1,951, about 2%.

                    
Bugs this caught: $s_0$ taken from hidden[0] (forward) instead of hidden[1] (backward); context set to the whole (2, B, H) hidden; the decoder calling the attention module even when it was off.

                    
Real data: this is RNNencdec. The paper's encoder for RNNencdec is unidirectional; ours keeps the Bi-GRU so only attention differs. State that in the write-up.

                


                

                    Step 5 • The metric
                    
Toy: greedy decode, then compare position by position against the target. Denominator is the target's non-pad tokens (words + <eos>). A prediction that stops early (early <eos>) counts the missing positions as wrong. One that runs to max_len has its extra positions ignored; that miss is already counted once, at the <eos> position. Buckets of 5 by source word count, plus exact match.

                    
Real data: token accuracy means little for translation. Use BLEU per source-length bucket, which is the paper's Fig. 2.

                


                

                    Plateau check • The confound
                    
Toy: after 10 epochs the baseline was bad everywhere, which would have "proved" the bottleneck for the wrong reason: an undertrained model is also best on short sentences. The baseline needed ~150 epochs (attention reached 1.000 in 9). Val loss is noisy in a ±0.1 band; best.pt at epoch 142 (val 1.000) was a dip below its neighbours (1.16-1.23). Quote the band, not the dip.

                    
Real data: train every model until no new best for ~5 evals. Long runs go in a headless script on the VM (nohup python train.py), because two notebook runs died when the Colab link healed and the remote Jupyter was restarted.

                


                

                    Step 6 • Is it just size?
                    
Toy: baseline at H=256 (1,150,988 params, 11.6x the attention model), 150 epochs. Mid buckets gain +0.2 to +0.28, but 36-50 gain only +0.03 to +0.08. A bigger H moves the reliable length from ~10 to ~25-30, then the curve drops sharply. A param mismatch in this direction is conservative: the bigger model is the one that loses.

                    
Why size is not the limit: 50 tokens from a ~100-word vocab is about 330 bits. A 512-float vector has far more room than that. The vector could hold the sentence; the model does not learn to put it there.

                    
Real data: the paper compares at equal hidden size, plus training on sentences up to 30 vs up to 50 words. Use that before a size sweep.

                


                

                    Steps 7-8 • Learning or memorizing?
                    
Toy (H=256): train minus val is ~0 at 6-10, +0.17 to +0.25 at 21-40, +0.11 at 46-50. Train/val loss gap ~2x (0.19 vs 0.37). Read per bucket: short sentences are learned; mid lengths fit the training set but do not generalize (memorization); 41-50 fail even on train (0.37-0.49), so the model cannot fit them at all. Attention is 1.000 on train and val.

                    
Real data: BLEU per bucket on a train subset of the same size as val.

What the toy shows

                

                    
• With a fixed context vector, accuracy falls with sentence length, and more training or 11.6x more params moves the drop to longer sentences without removing it.

                    
• The failure is not lack of room in the vector. Long sentences are not learned (low even on train), and mid-length ones are memorized rather than learned.

                    
• Attention gives the decoder a one-step path to every source position. With 1/11 of the params it gets 1.000 at every length, on train and val, in 9 epochs.
